# Voice and Clarity

## Active Voice

Write active sentences: the actor, then what it does. "The validator rejects the file" names who acts; "the file is
rejected" hides it, and a reader debugging the rejection needs exactly the part that was hidden.

Passive voice is correct when the actor is unknown or irrelevant — "the configuration is generated during setup" — or
when the action rather than the actor is the subject of the sentence.

Instructions use the imperative: "Run the formatter", not "The formatter should be run".

## Tone

Professional and plain. Respectful and inclusive, with no slang, no marketing, and none of the stiffness that makes a
reader translate — "it is incumbent upon the operator to" says "the operator must". Assume a capable reader who lacks
context, and supply the context.

Once a technical term is chosen, use it consistently. A synonym introduced for variety reads as a second concept.

## Concision

- **One idea per sentence.** A sentence carrying two ideas makes the reader find the boundary between them.
- **One idea per paragraph, and short paragraphs.** Lead with the point. A dense block gets skipped rather than read.
- **No filler.** "Basically", "actually", "simply", "just", "of course", and "it should be noted that" add length and no
  meaning. "Simply" and "just" also tell a struggling reader that the difficulty is their fault.
- **Define an acronym at its first use**, unless no intended reader could fail to know it.
- **Show a concrete example** when a rule is abstract. A realistic example teaches more than a placeholder named after
  nothing.

## Audience

Write for the reader the document is for. Learning material explains a concept before using it, proceeds step by step,
and defines its terms. Reference material assumes the foundation, gets to the specifics, and links prerequisites instead
of re-teaching them.

A document that tries to serve both usually serves neither: the beginner is lost in the specifics and the expert wades
through the basics. Split it, or link from one level to the other.
